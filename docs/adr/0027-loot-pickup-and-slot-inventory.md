# 0027 — Loot pickup and fullscreen inventory

## Decision

Loot is collected separately from manual equipping. The reward dialog lists pending finds. The first click on a find opens its description; clicking the same find again while its description is open moves it to the hero's persistent inventory. Clicking a different find switches the description without collecting either find. With automatic equipment selection disabled, pickup does not alter attributes or equipment. The optional [automatic equipment policy](0033-automatic-equipment-selection.md) evaluates collected gear immediately after pickup. Collected finds are available in the fullscreen inventory opened from the character control.

The "Забрать всё" button immediately collects the displayed pending equipment and abilities in one operation without requiring inspection. After pickup is confirmed and the collected finds have left the pending list, the dialog closes automatically if no rewards remain. This applies both to bulk pickup and collecting the final find individually. Pending pickups block repeated collection, and a rejected operation leaves the finds available.

The fullscreen inventory brings the complete bag, equipped character and set bonuses into one view. Clicking an equipment slot selects the replacement slot; clicking a bag item selects it for comparison. The first click on a candidate displays its description, the current item, and attribute/armor/durability differences. Clicking the same candidate again equips it immediately. Hands, rings and the two learned ability slots expose explicit replacement-slot choices. Switching candidates or slots never equips anything. Selection and comparison are shared between the bag and character views, including mobile tabs.

Displaced equipment and learned abilities return to the same hero's bag. This supersedes the previous destruction rule. Equipping a two-handed weapon returns both displaced hand items; replacing a two-handed weapon returns it once and frees the other hand. Each displaced ring is returned separately, even when both slots contain the same item definition. Other inventory entries remain available. Lost limbs and duplicate equipped skills follow core validation.

Bag entries can also be dragged onto their equipment or learned-ability slot. Valid destinations light up, and release revalidates the current inventory and body through the existing equip command. Rings and hand equipment use the exact destination chosen by the player. Dropping elsewhere, pressing Escape, or interrupting the gesture cancels without equipping; the release click is suppressed so it cannot trigger second-click equipment. On touchscreens, the item icon is the drag handle while the item text and surrounding bag remain available for scrolling. At mobile widths a temporary tray presents matching slots during the drag, avoiding a tab change while holding an item. Ordinary inspection and second-click equipment remain available for touch and keyboard users.

Closing the loot dialog with uncollected finds asks for confirmation before discarding only those pending rewards. Collected inventory is unaffected. Empty loot closes directly. Coins remain an automatic reward and a noninteractive HUD counter; only pending loot exposes an opening button.

## State and synchronization

`HeroProgress.inventory` stores collected and returned finds using the existing reward record shape. Old saves normalize a missing inventory to an empty array. `collect-reward` and `equip-inventory` commands run through the existing local/host/predicted command path and compact loadout events. Protocol version 14 prevents clients with the previous destruction behavior from deriving a different inventory from the same command. No new command fields or item definitions enter the wire payload.

Protocol version 19 adds `collect-rewards` with explicit reward IDs for bulk pickup. The whole selection is validated before transferring any finds; replayed IDs already collected or consumed do not duplicate inventory. Unselected rewards remain available. Both the client and relay must support this protocol version.

Returned finds preserve their catalogue rarity, use `loadout:<previous slot>` as their source and have no new luck rolls. Their short deterministic IDs include the hero's ID and skip every active reward/inventory ID and every consumed ID recorded in the run. Swaps by another hero therefore do not alter the local prediction's returned IDs. Repeated swapping never reuses a consumed command ID; equal item definitions in separate slots remain separate instances. Pickup/equip replay cannot duplicate them. The legacy direct-equip, direct-learn and reward-confirmation paths apply the same return rule. Closing pending loot never discards returned inventory entries.

`previewInventoryEquip` shares validation between presentation and committed execution. Changing gear preserves current limb-health ratios and lost parts; collecting loot and equipping abilities do not rescale health.

## Balance impact

Keeping displaced items and learned skills increases future equipment choices and changes campaign balance. Existing simulation reports remain historical results for their recorded rules; this change has not been recalibrated by those reports.

## Presentation

The inventory occupies the viewport. On wide desktop screens, the complete bag is on the left, equipped items, body condition, attributes and all five ability slots are in the center, and set bonuses are on the right. Between 701 and 1040 px, the set panel moves below the character in the right column. Long lists and descriptions scroll inside their own areas. At widths of 700 px or less, two tabs, "Сумка" and "Персонаж", replace the columns; the set-bonus panel is included in "Персонаж". Switching tabs preserves the selected item, replacement slot and comparison. Short viewports allow the workspace to scroll rather than clipping its bag controls or comparison.

The bag groups every collected entry into helmets, armor, gloves, pants, boots, weapons, shields, amulets, rings or skills. It offers sorting by rarity, newest arrival or set. Rarity sorting puts legendary → epic → rare → common, with newest arrivals first within a rarity. The saved inventory remains in chronological arrival order; presentation sorts a copy, so ordering survives reloads without wall-clock timestamps. Returned equipment counts as a new arrival in the bag. Selecting a candidate keeps current and proposed item descriptions visible together, with attribute, armor and maximum limb-durability changes. Repeated selection equips it; the displaced item then appears in its bag group. Empty equipment cells are dimmed. Body details and effect explanations remain available within the character view.

The set-bonus panel shows all equipped sets and the inspected candidate's set, with every 2/4/6 threshold expanded. Names, descriptions, aura icons and dice explanations stay visible without opening individual item descriptions. Active, inactive, gained and lost bonuses are distinguished, and participating piece icons identify the outfit's contributions. The selected set is highlighted. Duplicates and inactive equipment follow the same counting rules as combat; losing a usable piece updates the active thresholds. Previewing a replacement shows its gained and lost set contributions before confirmation.

The fullscreen presentation uses the existing collection and equipment commands without new protocol fields. `artifacts/reward-fitting-preview.html` demonstrates pickup, persistence and inventory equipment using production co-op commands in an isolated, unsaved workshop. Adding `?inventory=1` opens the fullscreen inventory immediately with mixed rarities, skills and two complete sets available for comparison.
